\section{Policy}
This ledger tracks arXiv:2207.14599v1 statement by statement.  We distinguish
false printed statements, type/domain errors, local repairs, missing arguments,
and statements actually validated in Lean.  A corrected statement is never
silently substituted for the printed one: the repair is recorded here first.

\section{Detailed issues}

\subsection{The auxiliary node order}
The paper orders shorter nodes first and uses reverse lexicographic order on
ties.  For
\[
S=\{\varnothing,0,10\},
\]
the canonical tree/lexicographic map from $2^{<2}$ sends
$0\mapsto0$ and $1\mapsto10$.  In the source the printed tie-break has
$1\preccurlyeq0$, while in the target the length clause gives
$0\prec10$.  Thus the canonical map cannot preserve the printed order.

\paragraph{Lean.} \ok
\lean{DualTree.OrderAudit.canonical_order_counterexample}.
The local forward-lex repair is checked by
\lean{DualTree.OrderAudit.forward_order_repairs_counterexample}.

\paragraph{Proposed repair.}
Use forward lexicographic order among nodes of equal length.  Exhaustive checks
found zero failures for the canonical-order and interior tests in the tested
binary and ternary ranges.  This does not by itself repair Remark 1.

\subsection{Remark 2}
With the printed order,
\[
S=\{\varnothing,0,1,10,11\}
\]
gives a semi-complete example whose interior
$\{\varnothing,1\}$ violates the one-successor direction convention.
The statement should be reproved after the order convention is corrected.

\paragraph{Lean status.}
The concrete witness is certified by the executable Section 3 checker:
\lean{DualTree.BinarySkewAudit.remark2_witness_is_semicomplete_printed} and
\lean{DualTree.BinarySkewAudit.remark2_interior_not_skew_printed}.  Equivalence
of this executable checker with the final propositional skew-tree definition
is still \todo.

\subsection{Remark 1}
Two independent problems were found.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item The condition $m\le n-|s|$ permits concatenations reaching level $n$,
outside $b^{<n}$.  The evident safe bound is $m\le n-|s|-1$, modulo the
authors' intended indexing convention.
\item The claimed extension of every skew $T'\subseteq T$ to a complete skew
subtree of the spliced tree has a finite counterexample even with forward lex.
This needs a new statement, not just an index correction.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Lean status.}
\ok The substantive forward-lex failure is now certified in
\lean{DualTree.Remark1Audit.no_forward_complete_extension}.  The chosen
spliced tree and the prescribed intersection tree are separately certified
skew by \lean{DualTree.Remark1Audit.forwardS_is_skew} and
\lean{DualTree.Remark1Audit.forwardTprime_is_skew}.  The remaining task for
this item is not verification of the printed statement---it is false---but
identifying the intended replacement needed downstream.

\subsection{Span inclusion and Theorem 3 for one-letter alphabets}
The paper defines refinement using
\[
[g]_{\Lambda}\subseteq[f]_{\Lambda}.
\]
If $|\Lambda|=1$, every variable word evaluates to the same constant word.
For $b=2$, $n=2$, $k=m=1$, coloring by whether the unique variable root is
$\varnothing$ gives two colors, while every one-variable word is a refinement
of every candidate in the printed sense.  Hence Theorem 3 is false literally
for $\ell=1$.

\paragraph{Repair and Lean status.}
\ok The algebraic and tree-specific issues are now separated and both are
formalized.  The singleton-alphabet collapse is
\lean{DualTree.SpanAudit.singleton_span_subset}.  The concrete binary-tree
witnesses are \lean{DualTree.Theorem3Audit.rootWord} and
\lean{DualTree.Theorem3Audit.leftWord}; their singleton supports are
1-complete skew trees, their supports differ, and
\lean{DualTree.Theorem3Audit.unary_spans_equal} proves their evaluated spans
are identical.  Finally,
\lean{DualTree.Theorem3Audit.two_colors_below_every_candidate} certifies the
finite witness, while
\lean{DualTree.UnaryMainAudit.no_unary_eventual_bound} strengthens this to
the actual threshold-level failure: for every binary ambient height $n\ge2$,
a fixed two-coloring has two differently colored $1$-complete refinements
below every candidate.  Hence no eventual Ramsey threshold exists for the
literal $\ell=1$, $k=m=1$ statement.

Conversely, if the alphabet contains two distinct letters and every source
variable occurs, then
\lean{DualTree.SpanAudit.span_subset_iff_substitution} proves that evaluated
span inclusion is equivalent to uniform syntactic substitution.  For the
tree-variable-word structure,
\lean{DualTree.VariableWord.support_subset_of_span_subset} further proves
that this refinement preserves variable roots/support.

Thus the span-based formulation is sound for $|\Lambda|\ge2$.  For
$|\Lambda|=1$ the literal Theorem~3 is false; defining refinement
syntactically instead would remove this degeneracy without excluding unary
alphabets.

\subsection{Corollary 22}
The rendered PDF is essential here because the text extractor repeatedly drops
equality bars.  Definition~12 defines
\operatorname{MTHJ} as the least positive $n_0$ for which the mixed-product
conclusion holds for every $n\ge n_0$, Theorem~10 likewise uses
$n\ge n_0$ in the definition of \operatorname{CT}, and Corollary~22 assumes
\[
d_0+d_1+d_2\ge1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
n\ge
\operatorname{MTHJ}\bigl(d_0,d_1,d_2,b,\ell,
\operatorname{CT}(1,k,b,d_2,r),r\bigr).
\]
Thus the height-one obstruction below is not an artefact of a strict/non-strict
threshold mismatch; it is a genuine counterexample to the displayed
Corollary~22.

Take
\[
d_0=d_1=0,\quad d_2=1,\quad b=\ell=r=2,\quad k=n=1,
\]
and color $(w,\varnothing)$ by $w(\varnothing)$.  The marked point is forced
to be the root, so the coloring is smooth with $\Gamma_1=D_2$.  Every genuine
one-dimensional candidate has both colors in its starred span.  At the same
time the canonical height-one subspace is $c$-good for every coloring, so the
relevant mixed-product threshold is $1$; and
$\operatorname{CT}(1,1,2,1,r)=1$ is immediate because the ambient vector tree
at height one has only the root singleton.  Hence the hypotheses of the
printed corollary really do include this bad case.

\paragraph{Lean status.} \ok
The height-one mechanism is source-facing:
\lean{DualTree.Corollary22Audit.color_is_smooth} proves the specialized
Definition~21 smoothness condition, and
\lean{DualTree.Corollary22Audit.no_monochromatic_complete_candidate} proves
that no complete one-variable candidate has monochromatic starred span.
Moreover,
\lean{DualTree.Corollary22Audit.canonical_good_for_every_coloring}
proves that at ambient height one the canonical one-dimensional subspace is
$c$-good for every coloring, the local fact behind
$\operatorname{MTHJ}(0,0,1,2,2,1,r)=1$.  The equally trivial CT identity has
not yet been wrapped in the general vector-CT API in Lean.

\paragraph{Likely repair.}
The obstruction identifies the missing resource in the usual proof:
$c$-goodness at one marked root can still leave dependence on the letter at
that root.  With two distinct variable roots one can keep the underlying word
fixed while moving the marked point, and combine smoothness with $c$-goodness
to eliminate this last dependence.  A natural corrected intermediate
dimension is therefore
\[
H=\max\{2,\operatorname{CT}(1,k,b,d_2,r)\},
\]
followed by
$\operatorname{MTHJ}(d_0,d_1,d_2,b,\ell,H,r)$.
This is consistent with the exhaustive binary height-two checks from the
pre-Lean audit.  The general corrected proof remains \todo.

There is a second formal boundary issue: Corollary~22 permits $d_2=0$ but
writes $\operatorname{CT}(1,k,b,d_2,r)$, while Theorem~10 assumes positive
vector dimension $d$.  The case $d_2=0$ should be split off or CT should be
extended by convention.

\subsection{The comparison after Theorem 25}
The displayed estimate
\[
\operatorname{PTGR}(l,m,b,r)
\le \operatorname{TGR}(k_l+1,m,b,\ell,r)
\]
fails already for $b=\ell=r=2$, $l=1$, $m=2$.  The right side is $2$, while a
simple coloring at ambient height two defeats the sole height-two candidate.
The qualitative reduction may survive with different parameters.

The notation $\operatorname{PTGR}(l,m,b,r)$ also suppresses the fixed alphabet
size $\ell$, although Lemmas 27 and 28 carry $\ell$ explicitly.  We should use
$\operatorname{PTGR}(l,m,b,\ell,r)$ in the formal development.

\subsection{Definition 24}
$I_S$ was defined for complete skew trees, but Definition 24 applies it to a
semi-complete tree.  The intended canonical transport should be defined
explicitly rather than overloaded.

\subsection{Lemma 16 and Claim 17}
The rendered source confirms that Corollary~9 is available at
$N\ge mq$.  The problem in Lemma~16 is therefore not strictness of that
inequality but the interpretation of the recursively defined $q_p$ as a total
block length.

For Claim~18, a type-correct cumulative recursion is obtained by adjoining
$q_{m+1}=1$ and setting, for $p=m,\ldots,1$,
\[
q_p
  =q_{p+1}
    Q\bigl(\ell,d_2^p,d_2q_{p+1},r\bigr).
\]
Then the stage-$p$ ambient length $d_2q_p$ is exactly
\[
(d_2q_{p+1})
Q\bigl(\ell,d_2^p,d_2q_{p+1},r\bigr),
\]
which is the amount required by Corollary~9 to produce
$d_2q_{p+1}$ blocks.  The displayed recursion in the paper omits the factor
$q_{p+1}$.  The same cumulative factor must be inserted in the starred
majorant recursion defining $q_p^*$.

Claim~17 has the analogous one-step indexing shift.  The clean formulation is
to adjoin
\[
M_{m+1}=1
\]
and, for $p=m,\ldots,1$, define $M_p$ by the paper's displayed MTHJ expression
with target dimension $M_{p+1}$.  Start with the identity subspace of
dimension $M_1$.  After processing coloring $c'_p$, the selected subspace
should have dimension
\[
M_{p+1},
\]
not $M_p$.  Thus immediately before stage $p$ the current subspace is
isomorphic to the corresponding mixed-product space of dimension $M_p$, and
Proposition~15 refines it to dimension $M_{p+1}$.  At $p=m$ this gives the
one-dimensional space asserted in Claim~17.  This simultaneously removes the
undefined $M_0$ appearing in the printed proof.

These repairs are bookkeeping changes to the iteration rather than a change
of its qualitative strategy.  The corrected qualitative simultaneous-
refinement lemma remains \todo in Lean; numerical bounds should be restored
only after that proof is formalized.

\subsection{Theorem 13 gluing}
The double-induction architecture still appears coherent, but the rendered
pages 21--24 confirm four literal source errors in the decomposition/gluing
formulas.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item In the definition
\[
\mathcal B
 = \{D'_2\subseteq \bigcup_{i\in D_2}J_i:
       |D'_1\cap J_i|\le1\text{ for all }i\in D_2\},
\]
the cardinality condition must contain $D'_2$, not $D'_1$.  As printed it
mentions a set which is not a parameter of the comprehension.  The intended
condition is
\[
|D'_2\cap J_i|\le1\qquad(i\in D_2).
\]

\item After Lemma~16 supplies
$(f'_z)_{z\in[d']}$ and
$(X'_z)_{z\in[d']\setminus D_2^*}$, one subsequent occurrence is printed as
$(f'_z)_{z\in[d]}$.  The ambient mixed-product partition is on $[d']$, so this
index must be $[d']$.

\item On page~24 the numbers $y^i_j$ are chosen in $[b^M]$ and
\[
Y_i=\{y^i_j:j\in[b^k]\}\subseteq[b^M].
\]
Hence the printed
\[
\overline Y_i=[b^k]\setminus Y_i
\]
is ill-typed; it must be
\[
\overline Y_i=[b^M]\setminus Y_i.
\]

\item In clauses (ii)--(iv) defining $f_i$, the source quantifies
$t\in b^{,n-M}$.  Since $s_y\in b^M$, concatenating such a $t$ has length
exactly $n$ and therefore lies outside the word domain $b^{<n}$.  The intended
tail domain is
\[
t\in b^{<n-M}.
\]
With this correction, $|s_y^rown t|<n$ and the gluing covers exactly the
nodes above level $M$.
\end{enumerate}

These corrections are local typing/indexing repairs.  They do not by
themselves certify the final implication that the glued
$((f_i)_{i\in[d]},(X_i)_{i\in D_1})$ is $(k+1)$-dimensional and $c$-good.
That final verification should be carried out after the corrected mixed-product
and Lemma~16 interfaces are formalized in Lean.

\subsection{Lemma 27}
This is the present structural bottleneck.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item With $n'=n-m'$, the map
$P_i(z)=I_T(I_T^{-1}(t_i)^\frown z)$ can leave the domain of $I_T$ for
frontier nodes at the larger intrinsic height.  A common safe tail loses one
level, or the cones need different tail heights.
\item The requested surjection from $\{0,\ldots,m'\}$ to all interior
variables can be cardinally impossible and can assign incomparable variables
to a cone.  Only ancestor variables visible from the given frontier cone can
be legitimate targets.
\item The enlarged alphabet
$\Lambda'=\Lambda\sqcup\{0,\ldots,m'\}$ is introduced but is not consistently
used in the mixed-product domain.
\item The induced coloring is defined on the starred part, while Corollary 22
takes a coloring of the full mixed-product space.  A compatible extension is
not supplied.
\item The reconstruction tests the occurrence position $P_i(z)$ rather than
the root $P_i(y)$ of the variable $v_y$, so different occurrences of one
variable fibre can be frozen differently.
\end{enumerate}
Therefore the final inclusion of every continuation of the fixed signature in
the monochromatic image is not established.  We should reconstruct the local
coding lemma rather than formalize these lines verbatim.

\subsection{Lemma 28 and Theorem 25}
Lemma 28 iterates Lemma 27 and additionally needs explicit transport between a
model signature in $b^{<m}$ and the corresponding signature observable in the
current variable word.

In Theorem 25, after pulling the signature-coloring back along $Q$, the proof
invokes the induction hypothesis without showing that the new coloring is
simple.  Tree-only finite tests are positive: the modeled $Q$ is onto in the
tested $l=1,2$ cases and tested simple colorings remain simple.  This should be
isolated as a lemma.

\subsection{Appendix Remark 3(ii)}
The unrestricted union assertion is false for disjoint $L_1,L_2$.  It becomes
true when $L_1\cap L_2\ne\varnothing$, which is sufficient for the actual
application $L_j=\{a_j,a_{j+1}\}$.

\paragraph{Lean status.} \ok
Definition~30's exact assignment relation is formalized as
\lean{DualTree.StarInsensitivity.StarRelated}, and
\lean{DualTree.StarInsensitivity.starRelated_iff} identifies it with the
three printed clauses.  The unrestricted printed claim is refuted by the
kernel-checked
\lean{DualTree.StarInsensitivity.disjoint_union_failure}.
The corrected overlap statement actually used in the proof is
\lean{DualTree.StarInsensitivity.starInsensitive_union_of_overlap}.
Its proof normalizes both assignments through a common bridge letter in
$L_1\cap L_2$, making the suppressed transitivity explicit.

\subsection{Lemma 31}
The rendered PDF removes two OCR false alarms: Lemma~31 assumes $k\ge2$, and
the proof of Theorem~8 later assumes $k\ge3$.  Two genuine one-step index
errors remain in Lemma~31 itself.

First, at construction stage $i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$ the new wildcard occupies
the coordinate interval $[q_{i-1},q_i)$ inserted after the first $i-1$
variables.  It is therefore the wildcard of $v_{i-1}$, not $v_i$.  The error
is forced already at the final stage: since
\[
d_m=m+n_0-q_m=m,
\]
the word $w_m$ has precisely the variables
$v_0,\ldots,v_{m-1}$, so a wildcard $v_m$ cannot occur.

Second, with
\[
d_{i-1}=(i-1)+n_0-q_{i-1}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
p_i=q_i-q_{i-1},
\]
the set
\[
J=\{0,\ldots,d_{i-1}-1\}
   \setminus [i-1,i-1+p_i)
\]
has cardinality
\[
\begin{aligned}
|J|
 &=d_{i-1}-p_i \\
 &=(i-1)+n_0-q_i \\
 &=(i-1)+f_1(k,\kappa,m-i,m,r),
\end{aligned}
\]
using equation~(8).  Thus the printed $m-i-1$ must be $m-i$.  With this
correction the exponent in (10) matches equation~(9), so the pigeonhole bound
has the intended value $p_i$.

Both corrections are local to the indexing of the Shelah-style construction;
nothing else in the mechanism needs to change at this point.  A source-faithful
Lean formalization of Lemma~31 remains \todo.

\section{Formalization order}
\begin{enumerate}
\item finite homogeneous trees, prefix and the two candidate auxiliary orders;
\item skew, complete skew and semi-complete trees, followed by Remarks 1--2;
\item variable words, evaluation, syntactic substitution and span inclusion;
\item the $|\Lambda|\ge2$ substitution/refinement lemma and the unary
counterexample;
\item starred insensitivity and the Appendix argument;
\item mixed products and Theorem 13;
\item signatures and a repaired Lemma 27;
\item Lemma 28 and Theorem 25;
\item Theorem 3 and finally the product theorem.
\end{enumerate}

Qualitative statements take priority over the large recursive bounds until the
underlying constructions are type-correct.
