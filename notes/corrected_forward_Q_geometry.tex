\paragraph{Scope.}
This note states a \emph{corrected and explicitly conditional}
version of the tree-support component of Lemma~27
(arXiv:2207.14599v1).
It is not an erratum for the entire paper and does not
prove its Ramsey conclusion. In particular, the paper's
\emph{specific} mixed-cone word \(g_w\), its induced
colouring pullback, and the original recursive bounds
are not established here. A conditional, type-correct
three-way word assembly and exact algebraic smoothness
criterion are verified separately, as summarized below.
The printed length-first reverse-lexicographic order and
unrestricted leaf replacement have separately documented
counterexamples in
\texttt{notes/lemma27\_Q\_counterexample.tex}.
The development below uses the globally \emph{forward}
length-first auxiliary order consistently.

\section*{Data and precise hypothesis}

Let \(b>0\), and let \(S\) be a finite semi-complete skew
source support with its last interior vertex \(t_0\)
in length-first forward auxiliary order.
The original starred source word has variable roots
\(\Int(S)\).
Let \(T\) be a finite \(k\)-complete skew support under
the same forward convention, with \(S\subseteq T\) and
\(t_0\in T\).
Write \(m=\htT(t_0)\), and assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:early}
 m+1<k.
\end{equation}
The repaired signature has a literal marker set \(R\)
comprising (a) the first exit boundary of each exceptional
terminal \(t\in S\) not extending \(t_0\), and (b) each
ambient child \(t_0^\frown i\) for \(i<b\).
Every marker has a unique minimal \(T\)-frontier \(f\)
outside the \emph{inclusive} forward cut at \(t_0\).
The marker-to-frontier assignment is injective.
Enumerate \emph{all} such minimal frontiers in increasing
forward auxiliary order, and let \(D_2\) be the finite
subset of indices assigned to markers in \(R\).

For each \(i\in D_2\), choose a local marked point
\(s_i\in b^{<k-(m+1)}\). Let \(f_i\) be its selected
\(T\)-frontier, and let
\[
 P_i=I_T\bigl(I_T^{-1}(f_i)^\frown s_i\bigr),
\]
where \(I_T:b^{<k}\to T\) is the canonical
support-address map for the \emph{forward} complete
skew support.
The shortened common tail space makes every \(P_i\)
well-defined even when \(\htT(f_i)=m+1\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projectrank}
 f_i\pre P_i,\qquad
 \htT(P_i)=\htT(f_i)+\len{s_i}.
\end{equation}
The actual reconstructed support is
\[
 Q=\Int(S)\cup\{P_i:i\in D_2\}.
\]

\paragraph{Starred \(D_2\)-order hypothesis.}
The only numerical consequence of Definition~20 used
by the geometric argument is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D2}
  i<j,\quad i,j\in D_2
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
       \len{s_i}\le\len{s_j}.
\end{equation}
This follows from a genuine vector \(1\)-complete
singleton family indexed by the \emph{selected} \(D_2\)
coordinates in their induced order. It is false to
assume \eqref{eq:D2} for an arbitrary element of the
unstarred mixed product. The Lean development uses an
explicit order-reflecting enumeration/certificate and
does not silently identify it with every printed
\(W_*\)-input.

\section*{The matching of the two intrinsic rank bands}

\begin{lemma}[Exact leaf/frontier rank matching]
\label{lem:bands}
For every actual marked projection \(P_i\) one of the
following two alternatives holds:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \bigl(\htQ(P_i),\htT(f_i)\bigr)
      &=(\htS(t_0),\htT(t_0)),\\
 \text{or}\qquad
 \bigl(\htQ(P_i),\htT(f_i)\bigr)
      &=(\htS(t_0)+1,\htT(t_0)+1).
\end{aligned}
\]
The alternative is independent of the local point \(s_i\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
All new projected \(P_i\) are terminal in \(Q\),
and the actual old interior is preserved:
\(\Int(Q)=\Int(S)\). Consequently \(\htQ(P_i)\)
is the number of strict old-interior predecessors of
its literal marker.

For an exceptional terminal \(t\in S\), the first-exit
boundary has exactly the original interior predecessors
of \(t\); thus \(\htQ(P_i)=\htS(t)\).
A minimal outside-cut source terminal has height
\(\htS(t_0)\) or \(\htS(t_0)+1\).

Its selected \(T\)-frontier \(f_i\) lies
\emph{on the original path to \(t\)}:
both \(t\) and \(f_i\) extend the literal first boundary,
so their meet is still outside the inclusive cut.
Meet-closure of \(T\) and minimality of \(f_i\)
force \(f_i\pre t\).

If \(\htS(t)=\htS(t_0)+1\), choose the genuine
source-support ancestor \(u\pre t\) of height
\(\htS(t_0)\). The two same-height interior points
\(u,t_0\) are in incomparable cones and
\(u\lex t_0\). This lexicographic relation persists
to \(f_i\), and the existing strict boundary-length
argument shows that the first exit is longer than \(t_0\).
Were \(\htT(f_i)=\htT(t_0)\), the \(T\) skew
same-rank clause would put \(f_i\fwd t_0\),
contradicting its being outside the inclusive cut.
The general frontier bound
\(\htT(f_i)\le\htT(t_0)+1\) gives the high alternative.

If \(\htS(t)=\htS(t_0)\), source skewness gives
\(t_0\lex t\). Take the \(T\)-support ancestor \(a\pre t\)
of height \(\htT(t_0)\). It is incomparable with \(t_0\)
and lexicographically to its right, hence outside
the inclusive cut by \(T\) skewness. The frontier
\(f_i\) must prefix \(a\), or \(a\) would violate
its outside-cut minimality. Thus
\(\htT(f_i)\le\htT(a)=\htT(t_0)\), and the standard
outside-cut frontier lower bound gives equality.

Finally, if the literal marker is the direct child
\(t_0^\frown j\), then \(t_0\) is itself a strict
predecessor in \(Q\), giving
\(\htQ(P_i)=\htS(t_0)+1\).
Its selected frontier extends \(t_0^\frown j\),
hence has \(\htT(f_i)>\htT(t_0)\).
The same frontier upper bound gives
\(\htT(f_i)=\htT(t_0)+1\).
These are all the literal markers.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Lemma~\ref{lem:bands} is stronger than the previously
known band bounds on \(Q\)-heights and \(T\)-frontier
heights separately. It proves that the \emph{same}
alternative occurs for each selected marker.
Therefore
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \htQ(P_i)<\htQ(P_j)
   \iff \htT(f_i)<\htT(f_j),\\
 \htQ(P_i)=\htQ(P_j)
   \iff \htT(f_i)=\htT(f_j).
\end{gathered}
\]
\end{remark}

\section*{Conditional semi-completeness}

\begin{theorem}[Corrected \(Q\)-support component]
\label{thm:Q}
Under the data above, including the explicit
\(D_2\)-length order \eqref{eq:D2},
the actual reconstructed support \(Q\) is a
semi-complete skew tree under the
length-first \emph{forward} auxiliary order.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The root, exact old interior, ambient meet closure,
\(0\)-or-\(b\) immediate successor counts, all
directions below the maximal cut, and final skew
branching witness are independent verified geometric
facts about this corrected \(Q\) support.
It therefore suffices to verify the two skew
rank/ambient-length conditions.

For two old interior vertices, both clauses transfer
from \(S\), since their intrinsic heights in \(Q\)
are unchanged. For an old interior vertex and a
marked projection, the equal-rank lexicographic
orientation and strict-rank length inequality
have been proved using the literal exceptional and
cut-child marker cases. A new marked leaf can never
have smaller \(Q\)-height than an old interior vertex.

It remains to compare two new projected points.
The genuine forward-sorted frontier list has
nondecreasing \(T\)-ranks by index.
If \(\htQ(P_i)=\htQ(P_j)\), Lemma~\ref{lem:bands}
gives \(\htT(f_i)=\htT(f_j)\).
The selected frontier cones are incomparable;
therefore \(P_i\lex P_j\) forces \(i\le j\).
The case \(i=j\) is trivial. If \(i<j\),
hypothesis \eqref{eq:D2} and the verified
monotonicity of canonical projections in the
sorted frontier order give
\(\len{P_i}\le\len{P_j}\).
This is the new/new skew same-height clause.

If \(\htQ(P_i)<\htQ(P_j)\), then
\(\htT(f_i)<\htT(f_j)\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:bands}. Frontier-rank
monotonicity excludes \(j\le i\), hence \(i<j\).
Using \eqref{eq:projectrank} and \eqref{eq:D2},
\[
 \htT(P_i)=\htT(f_i)+\len{s_i}
         <\htT(f_j)+\len{s_j}
         =\htT(P_j).
\]
The skew strict-height clause in the enclosing
complete support \(T\) now yields
\(\len{P_i}<\len{P_j}\).
This is the remaining new/new condition.
\end{proof}


\section*{Subsequent word and smoothness verification}

The corrected geometric result above has now been connected to
the \emph{actual} forward-sorted, selected-\(D_2\) starred
input subtype in Lean. In that subtype a vector-one-complete
ordered list of marked points gives the precise monotonicity
hypothesis \eqref{eq:D2}. This is a correction of the input
domain, \emph{not} a proof that the paper's printed
reverse-lexicographic \(W_*\) is equivalent to it:
the same marker-to-point assignment need not remain starred
after changing the frontier order. A typed binary witness
proves this failure.

A separately verified three-way interface constructs an
actual starred word on \(Q\) from a family of cone symbol maps
having three disjoint symbol types:
original letters in \(\Lambda\), auxiliary letters, and
genuine cone variables. Each auxiliary decoder and
cone-variable-to-source-root map must obey an explicit
root-prefix alignment condition. Under these conditions,
the cones are disjoint, the old source variable roots are
unmodified, variable fibres are uniform, and the resulting
word has \emph{exactly} support \(Q\).
Its evaluated letters are pointwise described by the three
cases of the decoder, with the original word used outside
the marked cones. This is a \emph{conditional} construction
of a valid \(Q\) word, not yet an identification with the
particular local maps prescribed by the printed Lemma~27.

There is also an exact normal form for
Definition~21's \(\Gamma_2=D_2\) smoothness relation on the
corrected starred mixed product. Write
\(\operatorname{patch}(x,r)\) for replacing the word of
every bullet coordinate \(i\) only within the inclusive
marked cone of its \emph{unchanged} marked point \(s_i\).
For corrected starred \(x,y\) one has
\[
 x\sim_{\Gamma_2}y
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{patch}(x,y.\mathrm{words})=y.
\]
Consequently a colouring \(d\) on the corrected starred
space is smooth \emph{for this all-\(\Gamma_2\) split}
if and only if
\[
 d(x)=d(\operatorname{patch}(x,r))
 \qquad\text{for all corrected starred }x
 \text{ and all replacement word arrays }r.
\]
This gives a \emph{sufficient special-case} test for
the particular colouring \(c\circ Q\).
Definition~21 only demands that there \emph{exist}
a partition \(D_2=\Gamma_1\dot\cup\Gamma_2\)
for which the colouring is smooth.
At \(\Gamma_1\), the full coordinate word must
agree but the marked point may vary; at
\(\Gamma_2\), the point and outside-cone
word must agree. The all-\(\Gamma_2\)
criterion must \emph{not} be imposed as a necessary
condition for every smooth colouring.
The general partition and its total-extension
criterion are now verified separately.
In particular, fixed-word colourings can be smooth
for \(\Gamma_1=D_2\) and fail the all-\(\Gamma_2\) test.
Even in that special case smoothness of \(c\circ Q\)
does \emph{not} follow just from invariance of
its support \(Q\).

A further verified construction fixes one letter
\(a\in\Lambda\) and replaces \emph{all} symbols inside
each marked cone with \(a\); denote the result by \(N_a(x)\).
Then
\[
 N_a^2=N_a,\qquad
 x\sim_{\Gamma_2}y
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad N_a(x)=N_a(y).
\]
Thus every \(d\circ N_a\) is smooth. Crucially, this
set-theoretic normalization can erase the original
cone-word information used for mixed-product spans
and Hales--Jewett substitutions. It must \emph{not}
be used as a shortcut for the Ramsey proof until
those image/refinement requirements are verified.

The source-facing Lean modules are
\texttt{ForwardQActualStarredSpace},
\texttt{ForwardQThreeWayConeWordAssembly},
\texttt{ForwardQThreeWayEvaluation},
\texttt{ForwardQStarredSmoothExtension},
\texttt{ForwardQStarredPatchCriterion}, and
\texttt{ForwardQStarredNormalization}.
The exact statements passed Lean CI and the transitive
no-\texttt{sorryAx} audit in merged PRs through
\#233 (921 audited endpoints). The later normalization non-surjectivity
and existential smooth-partition theorems passed CI in
PRs~\#235--\#236 (935 audited endpoints). The literal marker transport and the full-frontier
three-constructor word/evaluation interfaces passed CI in
PRs~\#237--\#239 (966 audited endpoints). The cumulative
\texttt{audit.tex} distinguishes these checked
conditional results from the remaining proof gaps.


\paragraph{Corrected source-marker partition transport.}
The literal corrected signature marker set \(R\) is
bijective with the actual selected \(D_2\) indices
of the \emph{forward-sorted} frontier list.
Consequently any partition \(R=B_1\dot\cup B_2\)
induces a genuine \(\Gamma_1/\Gamma_2\) split
on those mixed-product coordinates.
For a smooth-related pair, the entire local word
agrees at a \(B_1\) marker; at a \(B_2\)
marker, the local point and the word outside
that point's inclusive cone agree, so its
canonical projected \(Q\) node also agrees.
These transport identities are formalized in
\texttt{ForwardQMarkerSmoothPartition}
(merged PR~\#237, verified).

However, the paper's Definition~24
takes \(A\) to be the canonical terminal
frontier of the first \(l\) nodes under
its auxiliary order. This abstract canonical
\(A\) and the literal ambient signature
marker set \(R\) should be related by
a canonical structural/address identification,
\emph{not} assumed literally equal as
subsets of a common ambient tree.
Transporting the paper's \(B_1/B_2\)
simplicity condition across this
identification is still open.

\paragraph{The complete-frontier versus marked-point word distinction.}
The printed Lemma~27 defines the symbol
\(g_w(s)\) for source occurrences corresponding to
\(P_i(z)\), for every \(i\in[d]\) and
eligible local \(z\); this includes \(D_0\)
as well as \(D_2\) frontier coordinates.
The earlier conditional three-way construction
patched only the inclusive cones above
\emph{selected \(D_2\) marked projections}
\(P_i(s_i)\). These have different domains.
The new conditional
\texttt{ForwardQFullFrontierWord} instead
patches one root-aligned local symbol map
on each \emph{complete-support frontier cone}
rooted at \(t_i\), for every \(i\in[d]\).
This gives a valid global variable word,
with the unchanged old source roots and
inside/outside-cone symbol identities.
Its word component is independent of
the chosen \(D_2\) marked-point array
\emph{when all local decoded maps are fixed}.
That last qualification matters: the specific
local maps in the paper may themselves
depend on mixed-product data.

One further source-facing step is needed even
after this domain correction: the printed
clauses (i)--(iii) propagate constants and
old variables of the enclosing \(f\) using
the signature restriction \(g'\), rather than
simply taking an arbitrary preassigned
source word at outside-frontier positions.
The complete-frontier patching interface
does not yet encode this particular
\(f\)/signature transport, and it also
does not supply the prescribed \(R_i\)
decoders, Hales--Jewett span or colour
invariance. Those remain separate proof
obligations. The full-frontier patcher and its thirteen
source-facing results passed CI in PR~\#238;
the conditional three-way evaluated decoder passed CI
in PR~\#239.


\paragraph{Concrete visible-ancestor decoder at each repaired frontier.}
The next source-facing correction defines the auxiliary decoder
\(R_i\) for each forward-sorted minimal frontier \(f_i\)
using exactly the old variable roots strictly preceding \(f_i\)
in the ambient support \(T\). Its target is therefore the
\emph{visible root chain}, not all of \(\operatorname{Int}(S)\).
The proved forward frontier height bound gives
\[
 \bigl|\{v\in\operatorname{Vars}(g):
          \operatorname{root}(v)\sqsubset f_i\}\bigr|
 \le h_T(f_i)\le m+1.
\]
Choose a map from the \(m+1\) auxiliary indices
onto this visible list whenever it is nonempty,
and map them all to a fallback original letter
when it is empty. The resulting typed decoder
satisfies
\[
 R_i(j)=v\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{root}(v)\sqsubset f_i,
\]
so every auxiliary output in the \(f_i\) cone
is aligned with its original source root.

The module \texttt{ForwardQVisibleAncestorDecoder}
constructs these actual source variable witnesses,
the forward-specific rank bound and the decoder
with its coverage/strict-prefix properties.
It also replaces irrelevant local raw symbols
\emph{outside their own frontier cone} with
an original fallback letter. Since the global
full-frontier selector never reads these positions,
this masking changes no output symbol in its
actual cone. It removes the previous auxiliary
alignment assumption from the conditional
three-way Q word interface. The transport of
\emph{genuine cone variables} still requires a
separate explicit root-alignment hypothesis,
as do the source-specific raw words and
Hales--Jewett image/refinement claims.
The fourteen source-facing declarations passed Lean compilation,
finite regressions and the 980-declaration transitive axiom audit
in merged PR~\#240.


\paragraph{Capacity and necessary alignment of genuine cone variables.}
The remaining genuine cone-variable roots are not the
images of local tail roots under the complete-support
projection \(P_i\): those images lie \emph{below}
the frontier \(f_i\), whereas the output \(Q\)-word
must have roots exactly at the old source interior
vertices. Since no old variable root lies \emph{inside}
an outside-cut frontier cone, root alignment of
an occurrence of a genuine local variable \(z\)
above \(f_i\) is equivalent to requiring its
transported source root to be a strict ancestor
of \(f_i\). Equivalently, the root must belong
to the actual visible list \(V_i\) from the preceding
paragraph. If \(V_i\) is empty, no such variable
may occur in that cone in a legal output word.

Moreover, the actual visible list is duplicate-free,
has cardinality at most \(m+1\), and enumerates
the corresponding bounded old source variables
faithfully. Thus a family of \(m+2\) distinct
local variables, \emph{each actually occurring}
within the same frontier cone, cannot have an
injective old-source-root transport. This is
a finite-capacity limitation on preserving
independent local variable names; it is not,
by itself, a counterexample to the final
dual Ramsey theorem. Any proof that identifies
local variables must still establish the
required span/refinement properties after
that identification.

The necessary-and-sufficient visibility condition
and no-variable-in-empty-cone consequence
are verified in
\texttt{ForwardQConeVariableVisibility}
(merged PR~\#241); the bounded-list, faithful
indexing, decoder coverage and
\(m+2\)-versus-\(m+1\) non-injectivity
facts are verified in
\texttt{ForwardQVisibleRootCapacity}
(merged PR~\#242). Both passed the Lean build,
finite regressions and transitive axiom audit,
reaching 997 checked endpoints.


\paragraph{Existence of an aligned cone-variable root assignment.}
The finite visibility test has an exact constructive
converse: a root-aligned assignment to the old source
variable subtype exists precisely when no frontier
cone with an empty visible-root list contains a genuine
local variable occurrence. Indeed, if the visible list
at \(f_i\) is nonempty, choose its \emph{first}
old variable root and map \emph{all} local variable
names in that cone to it. If the list is empty,
no local cone variable may occur there; a genuine
old variable at the final source cut serves merely
as an unused total-map fallback. The resulting
full-frontier three-way decoder, with the already
verified explicit auxiliary \(R_i\), produces a
legally rooted starred \(Q\)-word with the
expected support.

This construction is intentionally non-injective:
all local cone variables at one frontier collapse
to a single old root even when more old roots
are available. The construction is therefore a
positive witness of \emph{legal output-word existence},
not a proof of the image or independent-variable
span needed for Lemma~27. The corresponding proposed
Lean module is
\texttt{ForwardQCanonicalConeRootChoice}; its six
source-facing theorems passed the complete
1003-declaration transitive axiom audit
in merged PR~\#243.

\section*{What is still not proved}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Relate the actual \emph{printed} \(W_*\) input
space to the globally forward-repaired starred domain.
Naive permutation of the selected \(D_2\) frontier
indices, while retaining their marked points,
is refuted by a typed example. One needs a new
joint transport of words and marked points, or a
corrected source-domain Ramsey argument.
\item The paper's original local cone height
and recursive numerical bounds must be reconciled
with the shortened common tail space in
\eqref{eq:early}, or an extra complete level
must be supplied and the iteration adjusted.
\item Identify the printed Lemma~27 cone words and
auxiliary-variable substitutions with the verified
\emph{generic} three-way \(Q\)-word interface.
Prove that the \emph{particular} raw symbols, decoder,
and variable transport satisfy the root-alignment
conditions and preserve the intended image/span.
\item Derive the \emph{appropriate existential}
\(\Gamma_1/\Gamma_2\) smoothness of the particular
induced colouring \(c\circ Q\), by transporting
Definition~24's canonical \(A=B_1\dot\cup B_2\)
simplicity witness to the corrected signature
markers and frontier coordinates.
Prove in addition that the particular full-frontier
decoded word preserves the required mixed-product
image, refinement and simplicity properties.
The abstract smooth-extension and normalization
lemmas alone do not provide these facts.
\item Lemma~28, Theorem~25, and the full dual
Ramsey theorem remain beyond this note.
\end{enumerate}
