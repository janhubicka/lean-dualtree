# Reduction of exact Q-to-output word equality to late original-f roots

Previous formalization:
* actual complete forward signatures identify the last cut and old interior;
* old/output dependent variable-root types are canonically equivalent,
  preserving EXACT underlying bounded nodes;
* the actual Q map reads symbols fibrewise from the ORIGINAL ambient f.

Over an alphabet with at least two distinct letters, any starred output
O whose evaluated span is included in the constructed complete-skew
final f' span is syntactically uniform on each original f-variable fibre.
This is an application of the independently verified
SpanAudit.fibre_uniform_of_span_subset and the actual f'⊆f span.
Original f constants also remain unchanged in O, by the generic
constant-preservation lemma. The actual corrected fibrewise Q output
has the same two properties without any additional hypotheses.

Thus matching the two dependent words after canonical root reindexing
at the ORIGINAL ambient f-variable roots is sufficient to match them
at every homogeneous-tree position. The full corrected signature
already enforces the early-root cases, including at the maximal
cut even though that cut is excluded from the strict partial-word domain.

The ONLY remaining word-symbol comparison is at late original ambient
f roots beyond the cut. On safe shortened cones this is a local
word/auxiliary-letter realization problem; on unsafe/no-frontier
fibres output O is already constrained by syntactic refinement of f'.

This reduction does NOT construct the mixed input w or its D2 marks.
It does not yet assert Q(w)=O as full starred objects: terminal
supports/tree geometry and the corrected starred W_* condition remain
independent obligations. For one-letter alphabets, syntactic fibre
uniformity cannot be deduced merely from evaluated-span inclusion;
the theorem explicitly requires the two-letter condition.
