# The remaining post-cut unsafe original f-fibre case

A late original f-root might have a selected outside-cut
frontier i while its canonical inverse address tail is too
long for the uniformly shortened local coordinate domain.

The crucial geometric fact is now proved via the already
checked ForwardInverseConeTails.projectCommon_reaches_target:
if the unique selected frontier i exists and that inverse
tail IS short enough, the original T root is EXACTLY P_i(z).
Therefore a root with safeAddress=none has either no
frontier or an unsafe intrinsic local tail.

Since all final f' variables are either retained before cut
or actual projections P_i(z), a late no-safe-address original
root is NOT a variable of the final interpolating word.
The honest total global substitution sends it to the fixed
fallback alphabet constant. The genuine fibrewise Q decoder
sends exactly the same fallback throughout its original
ambient f-fibre, by the already checked no-frontier/unsafe
cases.

For ANY old source assignment tau and ANY final assignment
sigma, actual Q and f' evaluations are equal on every
position of each such fibre.

Together with the previously merged safe late, early
(under anchored sigma) and ambient-constant cases, this
completes the mathematical case split needed for global
evaluated equality of Q(x_{sigma}) under tau with
f'(sigma) for the ANCHORED final assignment.
It does not imply equality for arbitrary independent
assignments to all retained final roots; anchoring is
essential. The Ramsey proof must also verify that the
direction of evaluated span inclusion is the one required.
